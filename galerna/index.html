How Galerna Moves

16 fixed bandpass filters across the spectrum, each with its own level. The sweep window, the tilt, the two filter slopes, the morph between two curves and the two halves gating each other all do one thing: decide which bands reach the output and how loudly. Everything below runs the device's own filter maths, so what you see and hear here is what the device does.

The strip is the device's own band display. Drag a column to set one band, or double-click it to put it back to 0 dB. With the strip focused, left and right choose a band, up and down change it, Home sets 0 dB and End mutes it. The faint curves behind the white one are the 16 bands on their own; the short marks across the columns are curve B.

The bank

16 bandpass filters sit at fixed frequencies, spaced evenly in octaves between the two ends of the Range. Each one has a level in dB, and the bank is the sum of all 16. With every band at 0 dB the sum is flat to within about 1.5 dB across the range, which is the point the Q is tuned to.

A band at −60 dB is off. Pull most of them down and you are left with a window onto a few frequencies: that is the sound of a filterbank rather than an EQ, because the bands in between are gone rather than turned down.

One band on its own sits about 5 dB below unity. Galerna normalises the bank so that all 16 together come out at 0 dB, which leaves each band holding its own share of the signal.

Sweep, Width and Depth

Width opens a window over the band numbers; Sweep slides it. At Width 0 the window is off and every band passes. Sweeping Sweep across the bank is the device's main move: it steps through the spectrum band by band instead of gliding the way a filter does. The window is a raised cosine, so a band fades in and out rather than switching.

Depth decides what the window does to the bands it covers, and it is what makes one control into two effects. At +100 only the window passes — a moving band-pass. At −100 only the window is removed — a moving notch, with everything else untouched. At 0 the window does nothing at all. In between, +50 halves the bands outside rather than silencing them, which is the difference between a sweep that cuts and one that leans.

Q

Q sets how narrow every band is. At 1 the bands overlap exactly enough that their sum is flat. Raise it and each band becomes a resonant peak with gaps between, which is more character and less neutrality. Lower it and the bands blur into each other, which sounds smoother and quietly raises the overall level because neighbouring bands reinforce.

Q matters twice over, because it also decides how articulate the vocoder is. At Q 1 a band is only about 2.5× louder than its neighbours two along; at Q 4 that is 31× and at Q 12 it is 155×. The flattest filterbank is the vaguest vocoder.

Tilt

Tilt leans the whole bank: band 1 goes to −Tilt dB and band 16 to +Tilt dB, with a straight line between them. It is the fastest way to make a sweep sweep darker at the bottom or brighter at the top without touching sixteen levels.

Odd, Even and the two slopes

Odd passes bands 1, 3, 5 and so on; Even passes the others. Each is a comb through the same settings, and switching between them on a sustained sound gives two voicings of the same material. Added together they rebuild the full bank exactly.

2p is the softer slope, with wide skirts and bands blending into each other. 4p runs each band through two stages, which halves the bandwidth and steepens the edges. In 4p the device flips the polarity of every other band so that neighbours still add instead of cancelling. Without that the sum would be full of deep notches. Switch between 2p and 4p above and watch the skirts, not the plateau: both stay flat.

Two curves and the morph

The bank holds a second set of 16 levels alongside the one on the faders. Morph crossfades every band from A to B at once, in dB — a band at 0 in A and −60 in B passes −30 dB halfway. A band that is off in both stays off all the way across. Press Store as B above, change the curve, then sweep Morph: the whole response travels between the two shapes rather than sixteen faders moving one at a time.

In the device these are eight stored slots rather than one spare curve, and the morph runs between any two of them.

The clock

Set Clock to free and the morph drives itself, one cycle every Rate seconds. Move decides what happens inside a cycle: ramp goes A to B and jumps back, swing goes there and returns so the cycle ends where it began, and step does not glide at all — it alternates A and B once per cycle, which is how you get a preset that changes on the bar rather than a sweep.

The device has a third setting this page cannot have: sync, locked to Live's transport, with the cycle set in bars. Everything else about it is the same. In the device the clock is a signal into the DSP rather than a timer writing parameters, so a four-bar morph costs no parameter changes and records nothing into an armed automation lane.

Cross

Each band is opened by the follower of the band eight along, wrapping round: band 1 by band 9, band 9 by band 1. What the bass is doing opens the top of the bank, and what the top is doing opens the bass. It needs no second input, because the input is both sides of the trade.

Turn Cross up with the chord playing and watch the plot move: the response is no longer a fixed shape but one the signal is redrawing continuously. On a full mix it behaves like a frequency-dependent ducking — a kick opens the air bands on every hit, hats open the low end.

On a part with energy in only one half it closes the bank almost completely. That is correct rather than a fault: cross gates a band by its opposite's envelope, it does not move energy up there, so it needs content in both halves to do anything.

The followers

Every band measures its own level, all the time, whatever the band is set to. That measurement is a follower: rectify the band, then let it rise at the Attack time and fall at the Release time. The FOLLOWERS row shows all 16 while the sound plays.

On the device's ENV page each of those 16 can be pointed at any parameter in the Live set, with an offset and an amount. The offset is where the parameter sits when the band is silent, 0 to 100 % of its range; the amount is how far it moves from there and which way, −200 to +200 %. Offset 0 with amount +100 opens from the bottom; offset 100 with amount −100 rests at the top and ducks. Above 100 % the follower is scaled up before it clips, which is how a quiet band still reaches the whole range of what it drives. At 0 the band lets go of the parameter. A seventeenth lane, SUM, is the mean of all 16 — the overall loudness of the part rather than one slice of it.

Two controls shape every follower at once. Decay tilts the release across the bank, as if every band had its own decay control and you turned them in a slope: negative holds the low bands and keeps the top snappy. Pre-emphasis tilts what the followers hear, never the audio, so a quiet top band can still drive something hard.

A long Release on a band with little energy is the easy mistake: the follower never falls back and the parameter sits still. Watch the bars before blaming the mapping.

The vocoder

The followers already measure the signal band by band. Point those measurements at a second bank fed by a carrier generated inside the device and the filterbank becomes a 16-band vocoder — no sidechain and no second track. The carrier is a sawtooth, noise, or both; Shift moves the formants by up to fifteen bands, so each carrier band is opened by a different input band.

Everything on this page still applies to it. The band levels, the two curves and the morph shape the vocoder exactly as they shape the filterbank, so a clocked morph becomes two vocoder responses alternating on the bar. Pre-emphasis is what makes consonants read, and the decay tilt is what lets low bands hang while the top stays crisp.

This page does not play the vocoder — it would need a second bank of sixteen filters and a carrier. Load the device for that.

Random, init and undo

The device's BANDS header has five buttons, and they are under the strip above. new draws a fresh curve, every band between −30 and +6 dB. vary stays within 6 dB of the curve that was there when you first pressed it. drift moves every band by up to 2 dB from where it is, so repeated presses walk. Rnd Amount scales all three: at 50 % a roll goes half as far. init puts every control back to its default, and undo takes back the last roll, init or 0 dB. These buttons run the device's own script, so a roll here is the roll the device makes.

On Push

With Galerna's track selected the 8×8 pads are the filterbank: eight columns are eight bands and height is level. Rows are counted 1-8 from the top, pads 1-8 from the left. Press a pad and that band goes to that row: row 1 is +6 dB, row 2 is 0 dB, then −6, −12, −18, −24, −36, and row 8 is off. The white pad on each column is the signal in that band while the sound plays. The pads above are driven by the device's own Push script.

Shift
The bars go dark and the pads are the menu. Row 1: pad 1 VIEW, pad 2 CONTROL, pad 4 bands 1-8, pad 5 bands 9-16. Rows 3 and 4: sixteen bar colours. Row 6: pad 1 dim, pad 2 bright.
Shift + Select
Tap Select: every band rolls the way you rolled last. Hold Select: the random page. Rows 1-2 are NEW, VARY and DRIFT, row 4 is Rnd Amount, row 8 pad 8 is undo.
Shift + Delete
Every pad turns white. Any pad: init. Let go and nothing is reset.
Knobs
The pads follow the knobs: turn a knob on the Bands 9-16 page and the pads show bands 9-16.

Mix

Mix crossfades between the input and the bank. At 0 % the input passes untouched, which makes it a clean A/B against whatever the bank is doing. At 100 % you hear the bank alone. In between, a narrow sweep window becomes a resonance on top of the dry sound rather than a replacement for it.

Things to try

Dub stab
Range 60–12k, 4p, Width 2, Depth +100, Q 1.5, Tilt −3, and Sweep on a slow LFO. You hear one group of the chord's harmonics at a time instead of a filter sweeping across all of them.
Moving notch
The same window with Depth −100. Everything passes except a travelling hole, which is a very different thing to automate.
Two voicings on the bar
Carve a curve, Store as B, carve another, then Clock free with Move step. The bank edits itself rather than sweeping.
Drone
Width 0, Q 3 or 4, a handful of bands up and the rest off. The bank stops being an EQ and becomes a set of resonators.
