How Abrego moves

Ábrego is the Spanish name for the south-west wind that brings the rain: it keeps shifting, and the weather never repeats. Abrego is a melody machine built from small, honest parts: two 8-step sequencers, five voices that add their inputs together, and two switches that step through sources. Give the sequencers different lengths, say 7 and 5 steps at the same rate, and their loops only line up every 35 steps, so short patterns make long melodies. Each stage below runs live, has the device's own controls, and feeds the next. Switch the sound on and turn things.

01CLOCKS→ SEQ 1, SEQ 2

Each sequencer has its own clock, locked to the transport: from 16 bars to 1/64, with dotted (D), triplet (T), quintuplet (Q) and septuplet (S) values, plus 1/5 and 1/7 bar. Swing delays every second 1/16. The bright ticks are loop starts; the line on the right says when both loops start together again.

TrySEQ 2 at 1/16S against SEQ 1 at 1/16: seven against four, on top of the 7 against 5 steps.

02SEQUENCERS→ voices, switches, accumulators

Two sequencers, eight steps each. A and B are pitch rows in semitones (0 to 24). T picks the trigger for each step: row 1, row 2 or none. V is velocity and P is the chance the trigger fires; the step still moves when it doesn't. F and L set the first and last step, so the loop can be any length from 1 to 8. Each sequencer sends four things on: its A and B values, and two trigger streams, written 1/1 and 1/2 for SEQ 1, 2/1 and 2/2 for SEQ 2.

TryDrag the A bars of SEQ 1. Click a T cell to cycle 1 → 2 → off. Pull a few P bars down to make the line breathe, then set SEQ 2's mode to ping.

03ACCUMULATORS→ rows A · not in Lite

An accumulator adds an amount to a row's total each time its sequencer lands on a step. The total shifts every value of the row, so a loop climbs or falls a little more each time round. At the limit it wraps, bounces or clamps; reset clears it every few loops. With scale the total counts scale degrees instead of semitones.

TrySwitch S1 A on: with +1 on step 1 the line climbs one scale step every loop, then bounces back. Click the top half of a cell to add, the bottom half to take away.

04SWITCHES→ voices

A sequential switch has up to four inputs and passes one of them on. Each pulse of its clock moves it to the next input. Clock it from a trigger stream, a sequencer's clock, or a loop (once each time the sequencer wraps), and a voice can play a different row every bar or every loop.

TrySW1 clocked by 1/2 instead of S1 loop: voice 4 jumps between rows on every second-row trigger instead of once a loop.

05VOICES→ MIDI notes

Each voice adds up three inputs. Every input has a sign: + adds, − subtracts, 0 ignores it. Inputs can be a sequencer row, a switch, Oct (twelve semitones) or another voice, so voices can follow each other in parallel or in contrary motion. The sum goes onto the voice's base note, is fitted to the key, and plays when one of the voice's trigger streams fires. Lit inputs changed since the voice's last note. Click a voice to edit it. In the device, the V1–V5 tabs open or close each voice's column; a closed voice keeps playing.

TryVoice 2 plays S1 A + S2 A. Set its second input to −: the same two rows, a new melody. Voice 5 already subtracts S2 A.

06MODULATION→ anywhere · not in Lite

Rows can move other things. A slot takes a source (a row, a switch, a sequencer's position, or a new random value on every step), scales it by depth, adds an offset, and sends it to a destination: pitch rows, velocity, first and last step, swing, gate length or probability. The scopes show what each slot sends.

TrySwitch slot 1 on: SEQ 1's position stretches the notes through each loop. Then slot 3: SEQ 2's row A moves SEQ 1's last step, so the melody's length follows the bass.

07PATTERNS→ everything

A pattern keeps every setting above. Click a slot to load it; press store then a slot to keep yours. Chain walks through a range of slots every few bars, forwards, backwards or at random. The dice roll new values per section; in the device you choose what each roll may touch, and auto can roll for you every few bars.

TryRoll the dice in SEQUENCERS, store it in slot 8, then chain slots 1 to 8 every 2 bars.

A simulation of the Abrego engine for learning, not the device itself. The device has more: rows A and B can run on their own clock divisions, steps can be skipped, the modes include CV addressing, the VEL section scales and humanises velocity, and patterns can morph into each other. Abrego Lite, the free edition, has everything here except the accumulators and modulation (and, in the device, the skip and mod rows, morph and auto dice). Settings here aren't saved. Every control is explained in the Reference PDF in the download; the Push cheat sheet shows what the pads do.
